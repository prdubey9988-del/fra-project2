Forensic Asset Quality Takeaway

ICICI Bank's GNPA ratio surged from 3.78% in FY15 to 8.84% in FY18 following the RBI Asset Quality Review (AQR) and the Feb 12 revised framework, exposing aggressive corporate consortium lending to power and steel. Under new CEO Sandeep Bakhshi from FY19, ICICI executed aggressive write-offs and pivoted toward secured retail, driving GNPA down to 2.16% by FY24.

The "Kitchen Sinking" Strategy (ICICI FY2019)

In FY2019, incoming CEO Sandeep Bakhshi took aggressive recognition to its logical extreme: ICICI wrote off ₹11,249 Crore and sold a further ₹2,845 Crore of bad loans (₹14,094 Cr in total), while fresh slippages fell to ₹11,039 Cr. This single-year purge wiped out legacy corporate stressed assets, clearing the way for ICICI's 5-year re-rating and return on assets (RoA) recovery.

Write-off + sale: ₹14,094 Cr GNPA drop: -7,771 Cr PCR reset to 71%
The Divergence & ARC Carve-Out (Yes Bank FY20 & FY23)

Yes Bank delayed recognition for years until FY20, when slippages reached ₹37,272 Crore in a single year. Recoveries could not keep pace. In FY2023, the bank sold a ₹48,000 Crore stressed-loan portfolio to JC Flowers ARC (GNPA reduction of ₹18,523 Cr via write-off/sale), resetting GNPA from 13.93% to 2.17%.

Risk Taxonomy & Event Horizon

Systematic vs. Idiosyncratic Risk Decomposition

Mapping external regulatory and macro shocks (Systematic) against bank-specific governance breakdowns and strategy choices (Idiosyncratic).

Systematic Risk (Sector-Wide Shocks)

Exogenous shocks that affect all financial intermediaries simultaneously. Cannot be eliminated via portfolio diversification. Transmitted through macro policies (Demonetization, COVID lockdown) and regulatory tightening (RBI AQR, Feb 12 Circular). Resilience is determined by capital buffers and pre-provision profitability.

AQR (2015) Demonetization (2016) IBC Code (2016) Feb 12 Circular (2018) IL&FS Crisis (2018) COVID-19 (2020)
Idiosyncratic Risk (Bank-Specific Breakdown)

Endogenous risks originating within the firm. Driven by managerial hubris, promoter concentration, internal underwriting standards, executive conflicts of interest, and deceptive accounting. These risks are theoretically diversifiable but severe when compounded with high leverage.

Academic Documentation & Governance

Methodology, Data Notes & Regulatory Framework

Underlying data architecture, regulatory classification standards, data break documentation, and empirical peer selection rationale.

RBI Regulatory Framework & 90-Day Norm

In India, Non-Performing Asset (NPA) classification is governed by RBI's Prudential Norms on Income Recognition, Asset Classification and Provisioning (IRAC):

  • 90-Day Delinquency: Interest or principal installment remains overdue for more than 90 days across term loans and overdraft accounts.
  • Asset Quality Review (AQR 2015): RBI inspection eliminated informal loan restructuring and forced transparent classification of stressed accounts.
  • Feb 12, 2018 Circular: Withdrew CDR, SDR, S4A schemes; mandated 1-day default reporting into the CRILC database.
  • Mandatory Divergence Disclosure: Banks must disclose divergences exceeding 15% between RBI audit and bank-reported NPAs.

Data Sources & Empirical Integrity

All quantitative metrics are sourced directly from verified regulatory and capital market publications:

  • RBI Publications: Statistical Tables Relating to Banks in India & Report on Trend and Progress of Banking in India.
  • Pillar 3 Disclosures: Bank annual reports, Basel III capital adequacy disclosures, and audited financial statements.
  • Investor Presentations: Quarterly earnings releases and management conference call disclosures.
  • Market Price Data: Yahoo Finance API (NSE: ICICIBANK.NS, YESBANK.NS, and peer tickers) adjusted for corporate actions and stock splits.

Data Breaks & Structural Events

Several structural corporate transactions occurred during the 10-year observation window:

  • Yes Bank Moratorium (March 2020): Reconstruction scheme led to equity dilution, AT1 bond write-down, and altered advance base. Post-FY20 reflects reconstructed entity.
  • HDFC Bank Merger (July 2023): Reverse merger of HDFC Ltd into HDFC Bank. FY2024 figures include merged mortgage assets (~₹24.8 Lakh Cr advances).
  • IDFC First Bank Merger (Dec 2018): IDFC Bank merged with Capital First. Pre-FY19 data reflects standalone IDFC Bank.
  • Bandhan Bank (March 2018): Listed in March 2018; historical data starts from FY2019.
  • Ratio basis: GNPA ratios match each bank's reported figures (GNPA as % of gross advances / gross customer assets). Gross advances are back-derived as Gross NPA ÷ reported GNPA ratio, so NNPA, slippage, write-off and credit-cost ratios share one consistent denominator. PCR is computed excluding technical write-offs (Gross NPA − Net NPA) ÷ Gross NPA.
  • Changing peer sample: The Yes Bank peer average uses 2 banks (Federal, RBL) for FY2015, 3 for FY2016–18 (IDFC First from FY2016) and 4 from FY2019 (Bandhan). Part of any shift in the peer average around FY2016 and FY2019 reflects composition, not credit trends. The tooltip shows n for each year.
  • Stock prices: Prices are split/bonus-adjusted (Yahoo Finance), so "Adjusted Price" levels can differ from historical traded prices; returns, beta and correlation are unaffected.
  • Flow-data verification: ICICI slippages (FY16–24; FY24 derived from annual-report write-offs, sales and recoveries), write-offs incl. NPA sales (FY19–24) and provisions (FY18–19, FY23–24) are matched to bank filings; ICICI recoveries for FY19, FY23 and FY24 reconcile exactly to reported figures. Yes Bank slippages (FY19, FY20, FY23; FY19–20 from the 2019-20 Annual Report NPA movement schedule) and provisions (FY20–23) are matched to filings. Remaining flow figures are compiled estimates; other recoveries are derived from the NPA identity.
  • Yes Bank FY2022 reconciliation gap: Opening GNPA + slippages − write-offs falls ₹30 Cr short of closing GNPA, implying slightly negative recoveries; the waterfall floors recoveries at zero for that year.

Peer Group Selection Rationale

To ensure valid economic comparisons, two distinct benchmark peer groups were curated:

  • ICICI Bank Peer Group (Large Tier-1 Private Banks): HDFC Bank, Axis Bank, Kotak Mahindra Bank. Shared characteristics: nationwide footprint, multi-lakh-crore advance base, corporate-retail mix.
  • Yes Bank Peer Group (Mid-sized Private Banks): Federal Bank, RBL Bank, IDFC First Bank, Bandhan Bank. Shared characteristics: second-tier private banks below the top-4 franchises, with higher cost of deposits and greater reliance on bulk/wholesale funding. Yes Bank grew faster than this group (advances ~₹2.4 Lakh Cr at the FY2019 peak) but is classed as mid-sized on market share (~2% of system credit), post-2020 scale and funding profile, which matched these peers rather than HDFC/ICICI/Axis/Kotak.

Mathematical Formulations & Formula Reference Table

Metric Exact Formula Economic Significance
Gross NPA Ratio (%) (Gross NPA / Gross Advances) × 100 Measures total recognized portfolio default rate under 90-day DPD norm.
Net NPA Ratio (%) (Net NPA / (Gross Advances − Provisions)) × 100 Measures unprovisioned credit risk directly threatening bank equity.
Provision Coverage Ratio (%) (Provisions Held / Gross NPA) × 100 Measures loss-absorption buffer protecting the bank against bad loan default.
Slippage Ratio (%) (Fresh Slippages / Gross Advances) × 100 Annual rate of new asset quality deterioration entering NPA pool.
Credit Cost (%) (Provision Expense / Gross Advances) × 100 P&L burden of provisioning; directly erodes Return on Assets (RoA).
Write-Off Ratio (%) (Gross Write-offs / Gross Advances) × 100 Rate of bad loan derecognition / transfer to bad bank or ARC.
